Glossary

Every term used in this documentation, in plain words, with the value this package uses where there is one, and the page that explains it.

ABCDEFGHLMNOPRSTUV

A

auto-clip
An option of step_total.py (and step 1) that cuts the along-strike columns step 3 found sitting on a different plate boundary, with a window derived from the mesh. kurilsjapan_v9 is run with it (the Izu-Bonin tail). The plate-boundary check
along strike
Parallel to the trench. The columns of the mesh are side by side along strike. The mesh
area-weighted mean
An average where each cell counts in proportion to its area; used for the zone's convergence. Convergence

B

b-value
The slope of the Gutenberg-Richter law: each magnitude unit up is 10b times rarer. From Berryman et al. (2015); kermadectonga2: 0.7, 0.95, 1.2 (minimum, preferred, maximum). LEVEL 1
Bayesian update
Re-weighting the logic-tree branches by how well each one predicts the earthquakes actually observed (LEVEL 3). The curves do not change, only their weights. LEVEL 3
Berryman et al. (2015)
The GEM report on subduction interfaces that PTHA18 used for literature values: seismogenic depth, coupling, b-value, largest observed magnitude. Read through lib/berryman_params.py. The input file
Bird (2003)
A global model of plate boundaries (PB2002) with the relative velocity of the plates along every short segment. PTHA18's source of convergence rates. Convergence
bow-tie cell
A folded cell whose four sides cross, like the two triangles of a bow tie; it has no sensible area or dip. The defect check counts them. Defects
branch
One complete combination of the uncertain inputs (coupling, b-value, Mw_max, GR shape): one possible answer, with a weight. 32,000 of them. Logic tree

C

centroid (of an earthquake)
The average location of the slip of an earthquake, as given by its moment tensor; can differ by tens of km from the hypocentre. Catalogue
clean ends
Step 1's rule that cuts a zone's end square along a down-dip line when the end would give cells more than 45 deg off square, or the zone narrows to a point; clean ends are kept. Clean ends
conditional probability
How the rate of one magnitude is shared between the scenarios of that magnitude. With Bird's convergence: in proportion to area x mean convergent slip of the scenario's cells. Conditional probability
contour
A line joining points at the same depth of the plate interface. Here: depth below the nearby trench, every 5 km. Contours
convergence
How fast the two plates approach each other, horizontally, in mm/yr. kermadectonga2: 99.36 mm/yr, area-weighted. Convergence
copula (comonotonic)
How the uncertainties of several segments are combined in LEVEL 5 (with --segmented true); without segments it has no effect. LEVEL 5
coupling
The fraction of the plate motion released in earthquakes (the rest slides silently). Uncertain: the tree uses 20 values between 0.1 and 1.3. LEVEL 1

D

dip
The angle of the plate interface below the horizontal (0 = flat, 90 = vertical). Mean dip of kermadectonga2: 15.42 deg (angle-weighted). Cell properties
discretizer
The method that cuts the contours into cells: optimal (default), lm or mid. optimal, lm and mid
down dip
Down the slope of the plate interface, away from the trench. The rows of the mesh are stacked down dip. The mesh
down-dip line
A line from the trench to the deepest contour, separating two columns of cells; rptha places them to cross every contour at a right angle. rptha's method

E

edge correction (LEVEL 4)
A multiplier of the probability of scenarios that touch an end of the zone, fitted so that the slip released along the zone follows the plate convergence. kermadectonga2: 7.15. A segment does not fit its own: it inherits the zone's. LEVEL 4
Esri, Leaflet
Esri: the provider of the map tiles (sea floor, satellite) of the report's map. Leaflet: the JavaScript library that shows them. Both load from the internet. The map
exceedance rate
How many earthquakes of at least a given magnitude are expected per year. kermadectonga2: 0.225 per year for Mw 7.2 or more. How often

F

FAUS
Fixed Area Uniform Slip: a scenario as a rectangle of cells, sized by the scaling relation, with the same slip everywhere. All rates are computed on FAUS scenarios. FAUS

G

GCMT
The Global Centroid Moment Tensor catalogue: every large earthquake since 1976 with its magnitude, location and fault orientation. The window used: 1976-01-01 to 2017-03-01. Catalogue
Gutenberg-Richter (GR)
The law that smaller earthquakes are exponentially more frequent than large ones, log10(rate) = a - b Mw. Two shapes are used: truncated (weight 0.7) and characteristic (0.3). LEVEL 1

H

HS
Heterogeneous Slip: a scenario whose slip is uneven, concentrated in patches, with the same moment as its FAUS scenario. Optional (step 7b); never used by the rates. HS
hypocentre
Where an earthquake starts. Catalogue

L

lm (Levenberg-Marquardt)
The least-squares method rptha uses to place the down-dip lines; also the --discretizer option that runs rptha's mesh exactly, without repair. lm
local datum
The reference depth of each point: the depth of the trench nearest to it. Contour depths are measured from it ("depth below the nearby trench"). Local datum
logic tree
All combinations of the uncertain inputs, each a branch with a weight. Logic tree

M

mesh
All the unit sources of a zone, as a grid of nodes: columns along strike, rows down dip. kermadectonga2: 73 x 3 = 219 cells. The mesh
moment balance
The rule that, over a long time, earthquakes release the moment the plates accumulate (area x rigidity x slip rate x coupling). It fixes each branch's rates (LEVEL 2). LEVEL 2
moment tensor, nodal plane
The description of an earthquake's source in GCMT; it gives two possible fault planes (nodal planes), each with strike, dip and rake. Catalogue
Mw (moment magnitude)
The logarithmic size of an earthquake: Mw = (2/3)(log10 M0 - 9.05). One unit more is 32 times more moment. Magnitude
Mw_max
The largest magnitude a branch allows. Its range is derived from the mesh as PTHA18 does, from the largest observed magnitude + 0.05 up to at most 9.6. LEVEL 1

N

NCI
Australia's National Computational Infrastructure, whose public server (THREDDS) holds PTHA18's published files: contours, meshes, cell tables, scenario tables, the saved R session. Validation

O

optimal
The default --discretizer: rptha's mesh, kept unchanged unless it has folded, collapsed or overlapping cells, and repaired only then. optimal

P

plate pair
The two plates that meet along a stretch of a boundary in Bird (2003)'s model, such as TO/PA (Tonga plate over the Pacific plate). Berryman et al. (2015) placed most segment ends where the pair changes, and --segment-boundaries bird starts a new segment there. Berryman, PTHA18 and Bird
percentile
The value below which a given share of the branch weight lies; the report gives the 2.5, 16, 50 (median), 84 and 97.5 percentiles of the rates. Uncertainty
PTHA18
The 2018 Australian Probabilistic Tsunami Hazard Assessment, by Geoscience Australia (Davies and Griffin). This package rebuilds its earthquake-source part. Home

Q

q (v10_q)
A rupture's mean, over its cells, of 1 / (number of ruptures of the same magnitude that contain the cell). With --rupture-size local it multiplies the conditional-probability weight, so places covered by more ruptures (smaller cells) do not get more rate. q

R

ramp rule (trench, v10_q)
A trench end more than 3 km deeper than the trench's normal depth is the edge of the SLAB data, not trench, and is trimmed (at most 100 km) before the depth-below-trench datum is built. --trench-ramp on by default. Step 1
rake
The direction of slip on the fault plane: 90 deg is a pure thrust, the upper plate moving straight up the slope. All scenarios here are pure thrusts. Convergence
rupture size: rptha / local (v10)
How many cells a uniform-slip scenario gets. rptha: one block per magnitude from the zone's mean cell size; local: a block per placement from the real km of its own cells. Cell-by-cell ruptures
rptha
Geoscience Australia's R package with which PTHA18 was computed. Python and rptha

S

s-matrix
How rptha describes the down-dip lines: for every contour, the fraction of its length at which each line crosses it. s-matrix
scaling relation (Strasser)
An empirical rule for the length and width of a rupture of a given magnitude; PTHA18 uses Strasser et al. (2010) for subduction interfaces. Scenarios
segment
A stretch of a zone, a run of along-strike columns of its mesh, that can break on its own, with its own convergence, coupling, Mw max observed and earthquakes. In v9 each is one segment of Berryman et al. (2015) Table 3.1, between the two trench end points the table gives it, and named as PTHA18 names the same segment (tonga, kermadec, hikurangi). With --segment-boundaries bird it is a Bird (2003) plate pair instead (to_pa, ke_pa). How v9 builds a segment
segmentation (LEVEL 0)
Whether a zone breaks as a whole or in separate segments. PTHA18 weights both at 0.5 and SUMS the segments within the segmented half. v9 reproduces this with --segmented true, taking the boundaries from Berryman et al. (2015) Table 3.1's segment end points, as PTHA18 did; without the flag every zone is run unsegmented. Segmented vs unsegmented, LEVEL 0
source zone (the LEVEL 0 mix)
A segmented zone's own answer: 0.5 x the unsegmented branch + 0.5 x the union of segments. Column exceedance_rate_source_zone of rate_curves.csv, and scenario_rates_<zone>_source_zone.csv per scenario. kermadectonga2_v9seg: 0.2521 per year for Mw 7.2 or more (unsegmented 0.2251, union 0.2790). Worked examples
seismic moment (M0)
The physical size of an earthquake: rigidity x area x slip, in N m (rigidity 3 x 1010 Pa). Magnitude
seismogenic cutoff
The depth below which the interface is too hot for large earthquakes; the deepest contour. From Berryman et al. (2015); kermadectonga2: 40 km below the trench. SLAB
shear modulus, rigidity (μ)
How stiff the rock is; M0 = μ x area x slip. Steps 7 and 7b use 30 GPa everywhere (PTHA18's rate_annual); step 7c (v11) uses PTHA18's depth curve, 10 GPa near the trench to 67 GPa from 35 km (variable_mu). What is new in v11
SFFM
The stochastic slip generator of Davies et al. (2015) that makes HS fields. HS
variable_mu (v11)
PTHA18's variable shear modulus case: each scenario's magnitude is relabelled with the rigidity at each cell's depth (variable_mu_Mw); the difference is treated as an error of the catalogue magnitudes in LEVEL 3, which changes the branch weights; the rates are variable_mu_rate_*. Slip and tsunami of every scenario are unchanged. How it works
SLAB2.0, SLAB1.0
The USGS models of the depth of subducting plates, on a map grid (0.05 and 0.02 deg). A zone whose PTHA18 name ends in 2 uses SLAB2.0. Downloaded from USGS ScienceBase or earthquake.usgs.gov. The SLAB raster
slab
The part of an oceanic plate that has sunk into the mantle. Subduction
strike
The direction of the top edge of a cell, in degrees clockwise from north, with the slab dipping to the right. Cell properties

T

trench
The long, deep groove of the sea floor where the plate starts to sink; the 0 km contour and the top edge of the mesh. Finding the trench
TVD
Total variation distance: how much the Bayesian update moved the branch weights, from 0 (not at all) to 1 (completely). kermadectonga2: 0.690. LEVEL 3

U

union of segments
The segments added up: on a segmented run, the second half of LEVEL 0. Column exceedance_rate_union_of_segments of rate_curves.csv. It lies above the unsegmented curve at small magnitudes and below it at the largest. What segmentation is
unit source
One cell of the mesh, about 50 x 50 km, the building block of every scenario. Unit sources
unsegmented branch
The zone as one fault. On an unsegmented run it is the whole answer (weight 1); on a segmented one it is half of it (weight 0.5), and the step cards, TVD, edge fit and PTHA18 comparison of the report describe it. Column exceedance_rate_unsegmented. What changes on the page

V

VAUS
Variable Area Uniform Slip: from an HS field, the smallest rectangle containing it, with uniform slip and the same moment. Optional (step 7b). VAUS
Values from the kermadectonga2_v9 example, and kermadectonga2_v9seg for the segmented terms.
