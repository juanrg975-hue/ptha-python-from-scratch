What the shear modulus is, and why it matters for tsunamis

The shear modulus or rigidity (μ) is how stiff the rock is. An earthquake's size as a seismometer measures it is its seismic moment, M0 = μ × area × slip, and its magnitude Mw follows from M0. For the same M0, softer rock needs more slip. Near the trench the rock is soft (about 10 GPa) and deep down it is stiff (up to 67 GPa), so a shallow earthquake of a given measured magnitude slips more than one at depth, and more slip near the trench makes a bigger tsunami. Steps 7 and 7b use 30 GPa everywhere (PTHA18's rate_annual); step 7c adds the depth-varying case (PTHA18's variable_mu_rate_annual, report Section 3.7.5).

How PTHA18 does it, and what step 7c ports

  1. Relabel the magnitude. Every scenario keeps its cells and its slip (computed with 30 GPa). Its magnitude is recomputed with the rigidity of each cell: M0 = sum(area × slip × μ(depth)). Shallow scenarios get a lower magnitude, deep ones a higher one. pyptha_v12/variable_mu.py: shear_modulus_depth, variable_mu_Mw (rptha append_variable_mu_variables_to_event_netcdf.R 90-114).
  2. Measure the difference (variable − constant Mw) on the HS scenarios of each magnitude and keep it as a distribution that depends on the magnitude (make_conditional_ecdf, rptha rupture_probabilities.R 1481; compute_rates_all_sources.R 476-516). On a segment only the HS scenarios touching it count.
  3. LEVEL 3 with an observation error. The rates stay functions of the constant-rigidity magnitude, the scale every scenario is built on. The GCMT earthquakes have real magnitudes, so the difference is treated as an error of the catalogue magnitudes when the branch weights are updated. That gives a second set of weights (posterior_prob_with_Mw_error, already in v9's engine but unused).
  4. The variable rates are the same branch curves averaged with those weights: scenario rates, their 5 percentiles, the rate curves, LEVEL 5 (MwRateFunction.with_mw_error; compute_rates_all_sources.R 749-869).
  5. HS and VAUS share the variable FAUS rates with PTHA18's DART curves for that case (a second pair, recovered from PTHA18's published rates like the constant ones). The peak-slip limit (7.5 ×) is the same.
What does not changeNo scenario changes: same cells, same slip, same sea-floor deformation, same tsunami. Only how often each scenario happens, and only through the LEVEL 3 weights. A zone with many GCMT earthquakes (and deep or shallow ones) feels it more; a zone without, like calabria2, little.

Rates per year (return period). calabria2 has no GCMT earthquake in LEVEL 3, so the two sets of weights are close (total variation distance 0.037): on the constant-magnitude scale the rates move between -6% and +4% at these magnitudes. Counted by their real magnitude the rates are lower, because most of the rate sits on the shallow rows, whose real magnitude is about 0.3 lower. Said the other way round: an earthquake of a given real magnitude in Calabria has more slip, and a bigger tsunami, than the constant rigidity gives it.

For tsunami mapsUse the variable rates with the HS scenarios (variable_mu_rate_* in outputs/hs_slip_fields/summary.csv), as PTHA18's headline maps do. Quote variable_mu_Mw when a scenario is compared with a real earthquake or picked as "the Mw 8" for a map: on calabria2's shallow rows a real Mw 8 is a constant-rigidity Mw of about 8.3.

How to use it

# from V9/ (venv ../.venv)
../.venv/Scripts/python.exe from_scratch_v12/generate.py calabria --zone calabria2 --folder calabria2_v11_varmu --ptha false --rupture-size local --variable-mu on
../.venv/Scripts/python.exe calabria2_v11_varmu/steps/step_total.py --skip-official
# or, on an existing v11 folder after steps 7 and 7b:
../.venv/Scripts/python.exe <folder>/steps/step7c_variable_mu.py --force

It is off by default: generate.py --variable-mu on makes step_total.py run step 7c after 7b (--skip-hs skips both); in a folder generated with off, step7c_variable_mu.py --force runs it by hand. Zones whose unit sources are normal faults keep the constant rigidity, as in PTHA18.

How it was checked

  • Against PTHA18's published files (validation/validate_v11.py): every published variable_mu_Mw of puysegur2 and kermadectonga2 (FAUS, HS and VAUS, 101,000+ scenarios) to 4e-15; the v11 engine on PTHA18's official puysegur2 input, with PTHA18's own HS catalogue, gives its variable_mu_rate_annual and all 5 percentiles to 7e-13 with every zero rate in place (and the constant rate_annual unchanged, 8e-13); every published HS and VAUS rate of both zones, both rigidities, to 4e-12.
  • The four DART curves (HS and VAUS, constant and variable rigidity) recovered from PTHA18's published rates: fitted on puysegur2 alone they reproduce kermadectonga2 to 2e-14; each is the derivative of the inverse of a cubic through (0,0) and (1,1), the method of PTHA18's report Section 3.6 (largest residual 4e-4).
  • calabria2_v11_varmu: every file of steps 1-7b identical to calabria2_v10_q's; step 7c checks that the engine's constant results equal step 7's before copying anything.
  • Tests (179 passed, 15 skipped): test_variable_mu.py (7: the rigidity curve and its log interpolation, the relabelled magnitude, the conditional ECDF against R's semantics, the rate function with the variable weights, the four weight curves, and PTHA18's published variable_mu_Mw when the files are present).

Known limitations

  • Only PTHA18's curve (Bilek & Lay fit, or PREM through "curve": "prem" in the input's variable_shear_modulus block). A Mediterranean-specific rigidity profile would be a new curve, not PTHA18's.
  • The depth is PTHA18's: the curve is applied to each unit source's depth, measured below the nearby trench (PTHA18's convention), while Bilek & Lay's depths include the water. PTHA18 does exactly this (its published variable_mu_Mw is reproduced to 4e-15), so v11 does too.
  • The DART curves come from 18 Pacific and Indian Ocean tsunamis (2006-2016); there are no DART buoys in the Mediterranean. PTHA18 applies them to every zone, because they correct the slip generator, not a place; using them for Calabria is the same assumption.
  • The segmented official check (kermadectonga2) was not run for the variable rates: the code path is the constant one's (validated to 1e-10) with the variable weights, and each segment uses the HS scenarios touching it, as rptha does.
  • Everything in v10_q's limitations still applies.
