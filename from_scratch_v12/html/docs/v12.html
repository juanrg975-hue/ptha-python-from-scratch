What is new in v12: an external mesh as input, and capped cells

v12 (2026-10-06) is v11 plus generate.py --mesh-file FILE: the unit sources are the cells of a mesh you already have, a text file with one line per subfault and its four corners as lon, lat, depth, instead of a mesh built from SLAB. Steps 3-9 (convergence, earthquake catalogue, logic tree, ruptures, rates, HS/VAUS, the comparison with PTHA18, the report) run on it unchanged. Since 2026-10-07 it also has --cell-size strasser: no unit source larger than the smallest rupture divided by k. Without the options (defaults: no mesh file, --cell-size mean) v12 is v11.

../.venv/Scripts/python.exe alaskaaleutians_v12/steps/step_total.py

The zone name still matters: it gives the Berryman et al. (2015) parameters (coupling, b, largest magnitude), the plate convergence and the earthquake catalogue window, and PTHA18's official run for step 8 if the zone is one of PTHA18's. --mesh-depth-units m|km overrides the unit detection (metres if any |depth| > 200).

Capped cells: --cell-size strasser (2026-10-07)

rptha's and PTHA18's rule sizes the unit sources on average: columns from the trench length / 50 km, rows from the mean down-dip length / 50 km. On a fan (calabria2: trench 981 km, 60 km contour 243 km) or a zone whose width changes along strike (antilles2, alaskaaleutians, kermadectonga2) some cells come out much larger: calabria2's trench cells reach 4042 km², more than a whole Mw 7.2 rupture (2390 km²), and some deep cells are 70-100 km wide. A rupture cannot be smaller than one cell, so there the local-size ruptures and the HS/VAUS footprints cannot take the size and shape the scaling relation asks for.

  • Use it with --rupture-size local: with rptha's one-block-per-magnitude rule the still-unequal cells give poor ruptures on arcs (calabria2: 31% within 1.5x).
  • Needs --discretizer optimal; cannot be combined with --mesh-file. --columns, the 50 km targets and fixed row counts are not used.
  • Not PTHA18's procedure: the scenarios no longer match PTHA18's one by one (rates per magnitude still compare). The largest gain is on fans and zones of varying width; on regular zones (makran2, kurilsjapan) any finer mesh gives the same.
  • Its effect on the tsunami hazard itself has not been measured.

Test: tests/test_cell_size.py. The 10-zone study: examples_newtest/html/report_strasser_meshes.html.

Known limitations

  • Only structured quadrilateral meshes (rows x columns sharing corners). Triangular meshes (e.g. NEAMTHM18-style) would need a different rupture builder, since the rupture rule and the HS generator work on rows and columns.
  • The mesh is used as given: if its trench is at 0.1 km (as this file) or its cells are uneven, the results follow it. For uneven cells use --rupture-size local (v10).
  • With --mesh-file the report's SLAB-specific parts (trench, contours, SLAB product) are left out.
