The 8 examples

Eight zones were run from start to finish with the current code, each chosen because it tests something different: a long arc, a huge one, a tiny one, a very flat one, one PTHA18 extended by hand, and three that PTHA18 never modelled with this geometry. For each one this page gives the command, what makes it interesting, its figures, its map and its numbers next to PTHA18's.

v9 folders, unsegmentedThese eight are v9 runs (*_v9), all unsegmented, each with its official step 8 where PTHA18 published the zone. Two of them also have a segmented twin, kermadectonga2_v9seg and kurilsjapan_v9seg, described with their numbers in Segmented vs unsegmented. Against v8's folders (*_v8) only steps 6 onwards can differ: v9 takes a zone's coupling from Berryman's "Whole margin" row where there is one, and its Mw max observed as the larger of Berryman's rows and the GCMT maximum, as PTHA18 did. That moved kermadectonga2, southamerica and puysegur2; kurilsjapan changed too, because it is now run with --auto-clip; makran2, calabria2, caribbean2 and antilles2 give v8's numbers.
How these numbers were producedEvery example was generated with --ptha false --discretizer optimal, so nothing is taken from PTHA18's mesh, and run with step_total.py --skip-hs. The numbers come from each folder's own files: run_final.log, outputs/, outputs_official/ and data/step1_contours_info.json. The script that collects them is html/docs/figures_src/make_examples.py.

Where the pipeline matches PTHA18On the four zones with an official run and the same along-strike extent (kermadectonga2, kurilsjapan, makran2, puysegur2), mean dip is within 0.6 deg and the rate of Mw 7.2+ earthquakes within 1.3%, except puysegur2 (see below). The GCMT event counts are identical wherever the meshes cover the same stretch.
Where it does not, and whyThe gaps come from the geometry PTHA18 edited by hand: southamerica's official mesh continues about 1,600 km further south than the SLAB1.0 data this package uses (and PTHA18 lowered its Mw max observed to 9.2, where Berryman gives 9.5), and puysegur2's official rate uses a fixed 35 mm/yr instead of Bird's model. Neither is a bug of the pipeline; both are explained in their sections.

kurilsjapan: cutting the tail with --auto-clip

.venv/Scripts/python.exe from_scratch_v12/generate.py kuril --zone kurilsjapan --folder kurilsjapan_v9 --ptha false --discretizer optimal
# steps 1-3 once, so step 3 can flag the tail; then the full run with the cut
.venv/Scripts/python.exe kurilsjapan_v9/steps/step1_fetch_slab2.py
.venv/Scripts/python.exe kurilsjapan_v9/steps/step2_build_grid.py
.venv/Scripts/python.exe kurilsjapan_v9/steps/step3_convergence.py
.venv/Scripts/python.exe kurilsjapan_v9/steps/step_total.py --skip-hs --auto-clip

Why it is interesting. The SLAB2.0 region runs south past the Boso triple junction onto the Izu-Bonin trench, another plate boundary. Step 3's plate-boundary check flags those columns, and --auto-clip cuts them with a window derived from the mesh, no coordinates typed. The result, 60 x 4 cells, has PTHA18's area to 0.2% and its Mw 7.2+ rate to 0.6%. Without the cut (v8's kurilsjapan_v8, 65 x 4) the mesh was 6% larger. Segmented twin: kurilsjapan_v9seg (numbers).

.venv/Scripts/python.exe from_scratch_v12/generate.py puysegur --zone puysegur2 --folder puysegur2_v9 --ptha false --discretizer optimal

Why it is interesting. The smallest zone (26 cells) and the steepest (36 deg). Two things make it differ from PTHA18 on purpose: PTHA18 extended its south end by hand beyond the SLAB2.0 data (this package does not invent geometry: its south end, 53 deg off square, is cut to 13 deg), and PTHA18 used a fixed convergence of 35 mm/yr here instead of Bird's model, which gives 25.6 mm/yr on this mesh.

calabria2: a strongly curved trench

.venv/Scripts/python.exe from_scratch_v12/generate.py calabria --zone calabria2 --folder calabria2_v9 --ptha false --discretizer optimal

Why it is interesting. The Calabrian arc in the Ionian Sea bends through almost 180 deg around a large, flat, noisy accretionary prism, and the SLAB2.0 data ends crookedly in the west (82 deg off square) and narrows to a point in the north-east (43% of its median width). It is the hardest case for step 1; the result is 20 x 5 cells with no broken cell. PTHA18 has no official run to compare.

caribbean2: the whole Caribbean raster

.venv/Scripts/python.exe from_scratch_v12/generate.py caribbean --zone antilles2 --folder caribbean2_v9 --ptha false --discretizer optimal

Why it is interesting. The SLAB2.0 "Caribbean" region covers more than one subduction zone. Without a clip window, step 1 takes the longest trench-like edge of the raster (2,300 km, from the Lesser Antilles along Puerto Rico and Hispaniola). This is a test of how the automatic trench finder behaves on a raster that is not one zone; compare with antilles2, the same raster clipped to the Lesser Antilles. No official run to compare.

Area 399,747 km2, mean dip 15.44 deg, convergence 15.65 mm/yr, 0 GCMT events, rate of Mw 7.2+ 0.0277 per year. South end (trench start): 86 deg off square, cut along the down-dip line from the trench's last point (242 km of the deepest contour removed). West end: kept.

antilles2: using --clip

.venv/Scripts/python.exe from_scratch_v12/generate.py caribbean --zone antilles2 --folder antilles2_v9 --ptha false --discretizer optimal --clip 297,307,5,18.5

Why it is interesting. The Lesser Antilles arc alone, cut out of the Caribbean raster with --clip LON_MIN,LON_MAX,LAT_MIN,LAT_MAX (longitudes in the raster's 0-360 convention). The clip window's corner makes the south end crooked (85 deg off square) and the north end narrows where the arc meets the window (46%), so both are cut. The flat dip (9.3 deg) gives 6 rows. No official run to compare.

Area 249,217 km2, mean dip 9.31 deg, convergence 18.50 mm/yr, 0 GCMT events, rate of Mw 7.2+ 0.0265 per year. South end: cut (242 km of the deepest contour). North end: cut where the zone is back to 70% of its width (105 km of trench, 203 km of contour).

Check the step 1 figure when you use --clipA window that cuts through a neighbouring slab is not detected automatically: for example --clip 283,307,5,18.5 keeps a strip of the Puerto Rico slab and no check flags it. See --clip.
